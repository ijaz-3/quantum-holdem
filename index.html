    WELCOME TO THE TABLE

    Same poker. New possibilities.

    Play ten hands against Nova. Each player starts with 1,000 chips and antes 10 per hand. The larger stack at the end wins. A player running out of chips ends the match early.

    Poker in 30 seconds

    Use the best five of your two private cards and five community cards. A pair means two matching ranks; three of a kind means three. A straight is five consecutive ranks; a flush is five cards of one suit. Higher categories win; within a category, ranks and kickers break ties.

    Check costs nothing when no bet is due. Call matches the rival’s bet. Raise increases it. Fold gives up this pot. Chips are play money. You need not measure to bet: you can bet on a range of possible hands.

    1. Read your cards. Your two candidate faces (different ranks, possibly different suits) are visible to you. Nova’s cards stay face down until showdown. Your first card is tunable; the second is linked to Nova’s second card. The community cards are ordinary cards.
    2. Choose two quantum moves. Mix amplitudes, shift phase, or measure either card. Z reveals and locks a complete card face; X and Y reveal a private phase-basis outcome and disturb the state. Each action costs one move.
    3. Read the link. Linked cards share a public contract: the same two candidate faces in different decks. They start on opposite branches, so learning yours can reveal Nova’s linked rank and suit. Mixing or measuring in another basis changes that relationship. Your panel shows only what your own observations let you infer. Neither player chooses a measurement outcome.
    4. Bet on your judgment. Check when nobody has bet; otherwise call, raise, or fold. Nova can do the same. Bets are capped at the smaller available stack, so there are no side pots.
    5. Finish the hand. After the river betting round, unresolved cards are measured automatically. The best five-card poker hand wins. Folded private cards remain hidden. Earned linked deductions do not reveal the whole opponent hand.
    6. Two physical decks. Identical faces from different decks are allowed. Five of a kind outranks a straight flush. Private cards reserve their two possible faces; no physical card is dealt twice.
    What is actually quantum?

    Each card has one qubit: |0⟩ selects its first complete face (rank and suit), |1⟩ its second. Z measures that complete-face basis. X measures plus/minus superpositions; Y measures +i/minus-i superpositions. Those are three different complete one-qubit bases, not three parts of one basis. Other directions are possible, but this game uses the standard three.

    The simulator retains complex amplitudes and density matrices, not just face probabilities. H mixes amplitudes; Z reverses relative phase; S shifts it by a quarter turn. Measurements project the state into basis states. A later face measurement uses that updated state.

    The shared Bell pair evolves under either player's actions. Private readouts condition only the owner's knowledge; the other player sees the outcome-averaged state. This prevents hidden results leaking through odds. Local actions cannot choose or remotely change the rival's unconditional outcome probabilities.
